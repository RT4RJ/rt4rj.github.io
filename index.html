Regulatory Technology for Fundamental Rights & Procedural Justice

Regulatory data and AI governance in the EU are increasingly supported by technical infrastructures: impact assessments, algorithmic registers, compliance ontologies, and digital oversight tools. This workshop asks how such systems can help make rights effective in practice, rather than simply recording obligations on paper.

§ 01  Motivation

Regulatory technology, under-theorized as an object of research.

Digital-rights legislation increasingly depends on technical systems that can represent legal obligations, assess risks, document decisions, and support recourse for affected individuals. Yet many of these tools — impact assessments, algorithmic registers, ODR platforms, and compliance ontologies — are still rarely studied as AI & Law research objects in their own right.

RT4RJ brings these infrastructures into focus through the expertise of researchers already working on data protection, ontology engineering for regulatory obligations, knowledge graphs for deliberative democracy, and access-to-justice research.

§ 02  Scope & Topics

Six areas of interest.

AREA_01

Fundamental Rights Enforcement Technologies

09 topics
  • Technologies for detecting, monitoring, and reporting rights violations
  • Rights-by-design in the architecture of regulatory and compliance systems
  • Rights safeguards encoding in automated public-sector systems
  • Tools for rights-holders: generating claims, gathering evidence, asserting rights
  • Rights indicators, dashboards, and measurement infrastructures
  • Public procurement as a rights-enforcement instrument
  • Documentation, logging, and traceability as preconditions for rights claims
  • Digital identity, biometrics, and civil registration systems
  • Rights protection in under-resourced enforcement capacity
AREA_02

Impact Assessments

05 topics
  • Algorithmic/AI impact assessment methodologies and standards
  • Fundamental rights impact assessments (FRIAs) for regulatory technologies
  • Data Protection Impact Assessments (DPIAs) in practice
  • Ex-ante vs. ex-post impact assessments
  • Distributed assessment responsibilities
AREA_03

Access to Justice

07 topics
  • Online dispute resolution (ODR) platforms

  • Digital divide and barriers for vulnerable/marginalized populations
  • E-filing, virtual hearings, and remote courts
  • Language access and accessibility in digital justice systems
  • Cost and efficiency trade-offs in tech-mediated dispute resolution
  • Disability-inclusive design in digital justice

Algorithmic Decision-Making, Transparency & Accountability

  • Human oversight requirements and practical limits
  • Profiling, scoring, and risk-assessment tools in public administration
  • Procedural safeguards against automation bias
  • Explainable AI (XAI) for high-risk contexts

  • Algorithmic auditing methodologies and independent oversight bodies
  • Corporate accountability for proprietary/black-box algorithms
